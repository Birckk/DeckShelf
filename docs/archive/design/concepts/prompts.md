# Commander Archive — visual concepts

Generated with the built-in image generation tool. These are exploratory mockups, not implemented screens. Generated card art, names, mana identities, and branding are placeholders; implementation will use source deck data and real mana assets.

## A — Living helix

Use case: ui-mockup. Generate one polished, high-fidelity desktop web application design concept, landscape 16:10. This is a local Magic: The Gathering Commander deck collection showcase named "Commander Archive", one user's 29 owned decks. Core requirement: the actual interactive deck view occupies 85–90% of the window, edge-to-edge immersive content; no marketing hero, no giant page title, no big introductory banner, no dashboard metrics, no permanent wide sidebar, no device frame, no explanatory annotations outside the app. A slim 48px top bar has small Commander Archive wordmark, search field, small view switch and collection button. Compact real-looking Magic white sun, blue water drop, black skull, red flame, green tree mana symbol filters and an "Exact colors" toggle in a small utility strip or floating control. A visible "Pick a deck" action belongs to the view. Modern premium readable typography, consistent restrained UI. Deck objects feature rich fantasy commander illustrations, readable short titles such as "Temur Roar", "Reign of Dragons", "Calling All Angels", "Keen Engineering", "Wretched Ranks", "Sultai Arisen", and tiny mana identities. Keep cards as the dominant source of color and visual interest. Use realistic Magic-inspired card proportions/art without needing legible card rules. It must look like an achievable interactive product screenshot, not a poster or a physical room.
DIRECTION A — LIVING HELIX. The page is a dramatic dark graphite three-dimensional card canvas. Two intertwined streams of portrait commander cards rise through the entire viewport along a tilted double helix, with 14–18 cards visible at different heights, angles and depths. Four foreground cards large and crisp, receding cards softened, asymmetrical composition showing the spiral's continuous movement rather than a horizontal carousel. Rich art, pale warm typography, restrained silver UI, a tiny amber interaction accent; avoid ubiquitous green-gold fantasy chrome. Give the canvas generous room while using the full height, not a tiny centered helix. A floating low centered control capsule holds small text "23 eligible decks", mana filters, and a compelling "Pick a deck" button. Tiny "Drag to rotate" hint; compact pause icon. The image captures an elegant energetic moment in motion with zero motion blur on foreground text. No giant central heading.

DIRECTION D — ORBITAL ATLAS. An immersive midnight-blue canvas containing an exploded spatial constellation of 18 portrait commander art cards, arranged along three broad interlocking elliptical paths sweeping from near foreground to far background. Composition fills the screen, not a small diagram. Cards vary in scale and gentle tilt with convincing perspective; large central selected portrait card "Reign of Dragons" is crisp, with nearby medium cards also readable and far cards small. Extremely subtle thin orbital guide lines, no astronomical stars, no neon sci-fi clutter, no connecting network edges. This is a playful draggable collection canvas, with a tiny zoom/pan control at the lower right and a compact bottom-center translucent dock for mana filters, eligible count and "Pick a deck". Silver typography, icy blue hover highlights and warm saturated fantasy art, sophisticated visual depth. Top slim navigation search and views. Keep functional UI legible and the page almost entirely the card exploration view. Unlike A's tall helix and C's rows, this has a radial map composition with a clear central focus and many surrounding paths to explore.
